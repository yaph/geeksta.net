Everyone you talk to
online is taking notes.

Every request your browser makes carries a small file's worth of detail about you: who you are, what you're running, and how you're set up. Nothing here leaves your browser except one lookup against a public IP database - everything else is read locally and shown only to you.

SUBJECT ID (derived from the signals below) computing…
low - common to many visitors medium - narrows you down high - identifies you specifically

Everything above comes from standard browser APIs (navigator, screen, canvas, WebGL) plus one call to ipapi.co to resolve your IP to a rough location. No data is sent to geeksta.net's servers, no cookies are set, and none of it is stored anywhere - reload the page and it's gone.

Canvas and WebGL rendering, screen size, timezone, and installed fonts are the signals that make browser fingerprinting possible even with cookies fully blocked. A privacy-focused browser (or extensions like uBlock Origin / CanvasBlocker) can flatten several of these to the same generic value for every visitor.
